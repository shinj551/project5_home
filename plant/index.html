FIG. 01 — ORANGE LANTERNS
SPECIMEN 001 / AUTUMN FRUIT

Chinese
Lantern

Alkekengi officinarum (syn. Physalis alkekengi)

As autumn arrives, this plant develops bright orange, papery husks that look like tiny hanging lanterns. Each delicate covering surrounds a berry, turning the plant into a natural seasonal decoration.

LOOK FOR
Glowing orange husks
SEASON
Late summer to autumn

Its lantern-like husk is an enlarged calyx, the structure that originally protected the flower. The plant is beautiful, but parts of it can be toxic and should not be eaten casually.

FIG. 02 — LATE BLOOMER
SPECIMEN 002 / AUTUMN FLOWER

Autumn
Crocus

Colchicum autumnale

These soft lilac flowers seem to appear out of nowhere in the fall, often blooming without visible leaves. Their delicate appearance hides a surprising fact: this plant is not a true crocus.

LOOK FOR
Petal-like purple blooms
SEASON
Early to mid-autumn

Its leaves usually emerge in spring, while the flowers appear months later. All parts of this species are highly poisonous, so it should never be eaten or mistaken for edible saffron crocus.

FIG. 03 — WOODLAND SECRET
SPECIMEN 003 / WOODLAND FLOWER

Ivy-Leaved
Cyclamen

Cyclamen hederifolium

When many garden flowers are finishing for the year, this woodland plant produces delicate pink blossoms with petals that sweep backward like tiny wings. Its patterned, ivy-shaped leaves often follow the flowers.

LOOK FOR
Upswept pink petals
SEASON
Late summer to autumn

Native to parts of the Mediterranean region, this cyclamen survives dry summers using an underground tuber. It often blooms as cooler weather and autumn rain return.
